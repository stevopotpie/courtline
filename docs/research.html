The backtest

The market already knew

Vegas closing lines missed the final margin by 10.55 points on average across 6,573 games from 2021–26. Courtline missed by 10.56. On totals both missed by about 14.2. The model agreed with the close so closely that it almost never moved a spread by a full point, and no group of leans cleared the 52.4% needed to profit at −110.

That is the expected answer. NBA closing lines are some of the most efficient prices in sports, and team form is already built into them. A backtest that reports this plainly is doing its job.

Rest

Tired road teams and the total

When the road team played its second game in two nights, games finished +1.23 points against the closing total across 1,158 games, about 2.2 standard errors from zero. Home teams on a back to back finished -0.65 points against the spread across 978 games. Both are suggestive and unproven, so they get retested on new games before they count.

Home court, season by season

Home teams have won by about 1 to 2.5 points a game in each full season since 2020, under the old 3 point rule of thumb, and the average closing spread has stayed within half a point of that every season. The market's average miss has held between about 10 and 11 points on the margin and near 14 on the total.

What comes next

Player minutes and props

If a model like this has an NBA edge anywhere, props are the likeliest place: thinner markets, many more lines, and slower adjustments to minutes and role changes. The plan is a player minutes model first, then props, then totals, with sides last if ever.

The obstacle is data. No public source has historical player prop lines, so Courtline will save its own prop odds going forward and grade early projections against results until enough market history builds up. Team form also knows nothing about injuries or trades yet, which is the first thing the next version needs.
