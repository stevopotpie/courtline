How the live chance works

The rest of a basketball game behaves a lot like a random walk. The live chance starts from the current margin, adds the pregame expected margin for the share of the game still to play, and allows less and less room for swings as the clock runs down. The weights were fitted on 2,885,589 real game states from 7,898 games (2019–26), using the closing spread as the pregame expectation.

Tested on 1,315 games from 2025–26 that it was not trained on, it called the eventual winner 71% of the time during the 1st quarter and 89% during the 4th. When it said a team had a given chance, that team won about that often:

Reading a card. To win is Courtline's chance that the team wins the game outright, the same bet as the moneyline. It is not the chance to cover. Spread is the pregame line, and Spread odds is its price, shown at the standard −110 (1.91x) since the feed does not carry each book's spread price; check your book. Spreads are set so each side is close to a coin flip, which is why a team can be 85% to win and still only an even bet against the spread. After the final, the odds column shows whether each side covered.

Brier score is the average squared miss of the chance (0 is perfect, 0.25 is a coin flip). What it does not know: who has the ball, fouls to give, timeouts, injuries during the game, or a team resting its starters in a blowout.
